# AXE Surface

**Version:** `axe/0.1`, Surface contract v0.8. Normative for `@axe/surface`; companion to [`AXE-PROTOCOL.md`](./AXE-PROTOCOL.md). Names are in [`VOCABULARY.md`](./VOCABULARY.md) under the Surface tables.

Changes: v0.8 adds a second granularity for the seam beside one action per control: `domainActions`, a fixed set of parameterized actions that each cover the entries the application selects and take the entry id, with the same act, revision, ladder, ledger, and events ([S-AXE-10] to [S-AXE-13]); and filters and pages on `<ns>.observe` in both granularities ([S-OBS-5]). Measurements and the reasons are in `docs/review/2026-09-24/16-granularity.md`. v0.7 adds an optional, feature-detected WebMCP exposure (`@axe/surface/webmcp`, §8): a page's controls offered as tools to the browser's own agent, never a sensitive field, acts through the page's surface, and nothing registered where the browser has no WebMCP ([S-WEB-1] to [S-WEB-5]). v0.6 adds `subscribe`, a listener for readers that mirror the table (a React external store), told after every change a reader could see, registration included ([S-OBS-4]), and states who owns a registration made by the React hooks: a change of the entry's `id` or of the target surface moves it, an act runs the latest render's handler, and Strict Mode's extra mount leaves the same table ([S-ENT-5], [S-DOM-1]). v0.5 runs the acts on one surface one at a time and checks a waiting act again when it reaches execution ([S-ACT-9], [S-ACT-10]), gives the handler the presented `expectedRev` and lets it refuse with a `conflict` of its own ([S-ACT-11]), and ignores a handler's revision that is older than the surface's ([S-REV-6]); and adds an authorization and privacy boundary to the seam (§6.1): the bridge declares `auth` on every action it generates and defaults to `user`, so a server without a `user` mode refuses it rather than serving the surface publicly ([S-AUTH-1] to [S-AUTH-3]); a server hosting many surfaces resolves one per caller from a fixed template ([S-AUTH-4]); an unidentified caller's availability never reads a protected surface ([S-AUTH-5]); a field can be `sensitive` and the ledger has a text policy ([S-PRV-1] to [S-PRV-3], [S-LED-1]); and `expose` is stated as browser-local ([S-DOM-2]). v0.4 names who owns the revision ([S-REV-1]) and makes freezes respect an application-owned one ([S-FRZ-2], [S-FRZ-3]); lets a handler report `changed` and `rev` ([S-ACT-1], [S-LED-5]); allows a `handoff` without a `url` ([S-AV-3], [S-AXE-2]); drops the action for a collection that cannot load more and moves the surface's own input checks behind the ledger ([S-AXE-6], [S-LED-1]); adds the live DOM mirror `attributesFor` ([S-DOM-1]); and keeps `@axe/surface` free of Zod ([S-ENT-3]). v0.3 serves every entry as its own AXE action ([S-AXE-1], [S-AXE-6] to [S-AXE-9]) and removes the generic `<ns>.act` of v0.2, which was never released. v0.2 added the ledger, the DOM mirror, freezes, and the ladder mapping.

AXE as specified in `AXE-PROTOCOL.md` is outside-in: a site declares actions for the world's agents, with discovery, requirements, handoff, and resume. Surface is inside-out: a running application publishes its **live** state, with reasons and one revision, and an agent acts through the application's own controls. The two meet at a seam defined in §6: every control on the surface is served as its own AXE action, with the control's availability as the action's, and its refusals map onto the escalation ladder.

Surface came out of two trials on business applications with opposite shapes (state in the browser; state on the server with a protected mutation protocol). Its rules are the ones those trials needed and no more.

## 1. Terms

- **Surface**: an application's registry of its own controls, plus a revision.
- **Entry**: one registered control. Kinds: `action` (a button, a switch), `field` (an input, a cell), `collection` (a list or grid that can load more).
- **Revision** (`rev`): one integer for the whole application, moved by every change an agent could observe, whoever made it.
- **Observation**: an indexed snapshot of the entries at a revision, with domain objects.
- **Act**: running one entry's own handler, guarded by the revision the agent observed.
- **Actor**: who acted: `agent`, `user`, or an identity the application supplies.
- **Ledger**: the surface's record of every act and every refusal, in order, with the actor and the revisions each spanned.

## 2. The three rules

**[S-RULE-1] Surface reports; it never decides.** An entry's availability and reason are whatever the application computed to enable or disable its own control. The surface performs no authorization, validation, or business logic of its own beyond the checks in §5.

**[S-RULE-2] Same door.** An act runs the control's own handler, the one a click or keystroke runs, so authorization, validation, and persistence stay where they are. The surface introduces no second path to state.

**[S-RULE-3] One revision.** Every observation and every entry carries the application's revision; every act presents the revision the agent observed; an act whose revision has moved applies nothing.

## 3. Entries

**[S-ENT-1]** Every entry has a stable `id`, a `kind`, a `role`, a `name` that states the outcome in the application's own words ("Draft Jahmyr Gibbs (RB)", "Lock March"), and optionally the `object` it belongs to.

**[S-ENT-2]** An `action` entry has `state` (§4), `effect` (§3.1), and a handler `(actor, text, context)`, where `context` is the act's `ActContext` ([S-ACT-11]).

**[S-ENT-3]** A `field` entry has a `value` and an `edit` descriptor: read-only with a `reason`, or editable with an optional `maxLength`, an optional `schema` for the text, and a setter `(text, actor, context)`. The `schema` is a JSON Schema, or a Zod schema (anything with `safeParse`): the surface validates a Zod schema by calling it and publishes it in `observe()` through the `toJsonSchema` converter the application configures (`createSurface({ toJsonSchema })`, passing `toJsonSchema` from `@axe/core`); registering a Zod schema without a converter is an error. The surface itself never imports Zod, so a browser bundle of `@axe/surface` carries none.

**[S-ENT-4]** A `collection` entry has `total`, `loaded`, `filter`, and `sort`, and is actionable only when it can load more (`more(context)`).

**[S-ENT-5]** Entries are kept in registration order. Replacing an entry by id keeps its position, so an index observed for one row does not move because a sibling refreshed. Unregistering removes the row; `reconcile(prefix, keep)` removes every row under a prefix that a re-scan did not keep. The React hooks (`useSurface`, `useSurfaceProps` in `@axe/surface/react`) follow the same rule: a refresh when `deps` change replaces the entry in place, and a re-render with unchanged `deps` registers nothing. The registration belongs to the pair of the entry's `id` and the target surface: when either changes, the hook unregisters the previous pair and registers the new one (at the end of the table, as any new id), and unmount unregisters the current pair. The handler, `edit.set`, `more`, and `requires.fulfill` the surface calls are those of the latest committed render, whether or not `deps` list what they read. Strict Mode's extra unmount and mount leave the table as a single mount does, in render order. Two mounted components that register the same id on one surface share one row, and the first to unmount removes it; applications give each control its own id.

### 3.1 Effect

**[S-EFF-1]** `effect` carries AXE's risk metadata: `mutates` (the name of the state the action changes), `idempotent`, `reversible`, and `risk` (`none` is never valid for an action; the default is `medium`). `undoable` is a deprecated alias of `reversible`. `confirm: true` means the act must carry `confirm: true`. `text` is the prompt for free text the handler accepts.

**[S-EFF-2]** Effect metadata MUST be truthful (protocol [P-HON-7]). An action that changes state MUST NOT be presented as read-only; the surface has no read-only action kind for that reason.

## 4. Availability

**[S-AV-1]** An action's `state` is `{ available: true }` or `{ available: false, reason }`. `reason` is plain language in the application's words, present whenever `available` is false.

**[S-AV-2]** An unavailable action MAY declare `requires`: an ordered list of `accepts`-shaped entries (protocol §8, kinds from `KINDS.md`) that would make it available, with a `fulfill(kind, payload, actor)` the application runs when an agent supplies one.

**[S-AV-3]** An unavailable action MAY declare a `handoff` (`reason`, `instructions`, `whatAgentShouldSayToUser`, and optionally `url`; protocol §9.1 without the token fields) for a step only a human can take. `url` names the page where the person takes the step; it is omitted when the step is taken inside the application itself (a menu, a dialog) and no page names it. An application MUST NOT invent a `url`. A handoff without one is reported ([S-ACT-4], [S-AXE-8]) and never becomes `needs_human` ([S-AXE-2]).

**[S-AV-4]** Availability text (`name`, `reason`, `instructions`, `whatAgentShouldSayToUser`) is agent-read text and is subject to protocol §18.

## 5. Observe and act

**[S-OBS-1]** `observe()` returns `{ protocol, app, rev, at, entries, objects, ledgerSeq }`. Each entry row carries `index`, `id`, `kind`, `role`, `name`, `object`, `rev`, and its kind's members. `index` is a 1-based position among rows that can be acted on now, and `null` otherwise. `ledgerSeq` is the latest ledger sequence number (§5.1).

**[S-OBS-2]** Unavailable actions, read-only fields, and collections that cannot load more remain in `entries` with their reasons. Agents learn why not before attempting.

**[S-OBS-3]** Three reads serve consumers that derive something per entry without taking a whole observation. `entries()` returns the registered entries in registration order. `registryVersion()` is a counter that moves on every `register`, `unregister`, `reconcile`, and `reset`, and on nothing else: a `bump` alone leaves it, so a consumer can tell "the table changed shape" from "the revision moved". `availability(id)` returns one entry's live availability as `observe()` would report it: an action's `state` with `requires` as `accepts` entries, a field's read-only `reason`, `cannot load more` for a collection without `more`, and the freeze's reason for every kind while frozen ([S-FRZ-1]); undefined for an unknown id.

**[S-OBS-4]** `subscribe(listener)` calls the listener, with no argument, after every change to what `observe()`, `availability()`, or `attributesFor()` can report: a `register`, an `unregister` or `reconcile` that removed a row, a `bump`, a `setRev`, a `seedRev` that adopted a revision, a `freeze`, a `thaw`, and a `reset`. It returns the function that unsubscribes. It is the subscribe function React's useSyncExternalStore takes, and it fires in `app` revision mode, where no `bump` happens. `onChange` keeps its narrower meaning ([S-FRZ-2]): it fires on `bump`, `freeze`, and `thaw` and not on registration, so a consumer that rebuilds per change of the action set (the bridge's `onChange`, and the MCP tool-list notification behind it) is not woken once per registered row.

**[S-OBS-5] Filtered, paged observation through the bridge.** `<ns>.observe` ([S-AXE-1]) takes an input of optional members: `object` (rows whose `object` equals it), `kind`, `available` (`true`: rows with an `index`; `false`: rows without), `query` (rows whose `name`, `id`, or `object` contains it, case-insensitively), `limit`, and `cursor`. Its output is the observation of [S-OBS-1] with `entries` narrowed to one page of the matching rows, plus `total` (the number of matching rows) and `nextCursor` (present exactly when more rows match). Rows keep registration order ([S-ENT-5]) and carry the `index` and `rev` they have in the whole observation, so an index read from a filtered page still resolves under [S-ACT-7]. The action declares `pagination` (protocol [P-AFF-7]) with `cursorIn` `/cursor`, `cursorOut` `/nextCursor`, and the bridge's page size: `limit` defaults to `pageSize` and MUST NOT exceed `maxPageSize` (a larger `limit` is `invalid_input`). Defaults: 1000 and 1000 for `surfaceActions`, so a surface of up to 1000 rows is one page as before; 50 and 200 for `domainActions`. A cursor is valid only with the filters it was issued for (otherwise `invalid_input` at `/cursor`) and names the last row it returned and the row after it: the next page starts after the first of those still registered, else at the position after the page. Each page is an observation at its own `rev`; an act presents the `rev` of the page it read. `surface.observe()` is unchanged and returns every row.

**[S-ACT-1]** `act({ op, target, text?, expectedRev, actor?, confirm? })` returns an AXE result envelope. On success it returns `ok` with `{ rev, id, actor, changed, seq }`, where `changed` lists the entry ids whose state the act moved and `seq` is the ledger entry the act wrote (§5.1). A handler, a field's `set`, or a collection's `more` MAY return (or resolve to) `{ changed?, rev?, conflict? }`: `changed` is then what the handler reported, and `rev` is the application's revision after the change, adopted as `setRev` adopts it before the result is built unless it is older than the surface's ([S-REV-6]); `conflict` is a refusal ([S-ACT-11]). When the handler reports no `changed`, `changed` is `[id]` if the revision moved during the act (by the handler, or by a reported `rev`) and `[]` otherwise. An application whose revision arrives after the handler (a server revision adopted later with `setRev`) therefore reports `changed` and `rev` from the handler or accepts an empty `changed`; agents SHOULD re-observe rather than read `changed` as the whole story.

**[S-ACT-2]** When `expectedRev` differs from the current revision, `act` MUST return `error` with `code: "conflict"`, `retryable: true`, and `details: { expectedRev, currentRev, seq }` (the protocol's stale shape, [P-LIVE-3], plus the ledger `seq` of the refusal), and MUST apply nothing.

**[S-ACT-3]** A missing `expectedRev`, an unknown `op`, an `op` that does not fit the entry's kind, a `confirm` that is required and absent, text longer than `maxLength`, or text that fails the field's schema is `invalid_input` with `details.issues`. An unknown `target` is `not_found`.

**[S-ACT-4]** An unavailable action or a read-only field is `error` with `code: "unavailable"` and `details.reason`; when the entry declares `requires` or `handoff`, those are included in `details`, the handoff with or without its `url` ([S-AV-3]).

**[S-ACT-5]** `target` is an entry id or an index from an observation at the same revision. Ids are stable and SHOULD be preferred.

**[S-ACT-6]** `target` MUST be a non-empty string (an id) or a non-negative integer (an index). Any other value, including a missing or `null` target, an empty string, a negative or fractional number, `NaN`, a boolean, or an object, is `invalid_input` with an issue at `/target`, and no handler runs.

**[S-ACT-7]** An index MUST resolve only to the entry whose `index` in the observation at `expectedRev` is that number. An entry whose `index` is `null` MUST NOT be reachable by number, whatever position it holds in the table; a number that no entry carries is `not_found`. The revision guard of [S-ACT-2] MUST run before the target is resolved, so an index observed at an older revision is `conflict` and can never land on the row that slid into its slot.

**[S-ACT-8]** A string `target` that names a registered entry which cannot be acted on (an unavailable action, a read-only field, a collection that cannot load more) is `unavailable` with `details.reason` per [S-ACT-4], not `not_found`. `not_found` is reserved for a target no entry carries.

**[S-ACT-9] Ordering.** The acts on one surface run one at a time, in the order `act` was called. Every check of §5 that precedes the handler ([S-ACT-2] to [S-ACT-8], [S-FRZ-3]) runs when `act` is called (the claim), so a refusal never waits. An act that passes them while another act is in flight waits for every act claimed before it and, when it reaches execution, MUST run those checks again against the state at that moment: a revision moved by a predecessor's commit or by a person (`bump`, `setRev`) is `conflict`, a freeze taken while it waited is `unavailable`, and a control that became unavailable is `unavailable`; no handler runs. `expectedRev` keeps its meaning: it must equal the revision when the act executes, so a second act presenting the revision a predecessor has since moved is `conflict` ([S-REV-2]). An act on an idle surface starts its handler within the call. Reads do not wait: `observe()`, `availability()`, and `ledger()` answer at once and report the state before a handler in flight has committed. A handler that never settles holds back every later act; a handler MUST NOT await an `act` on its own surface, which would wait for itself.

**[S-ACT-10] A commit that leaves the revision in place.** When an app-owned revision arrives after the handler (the handler reports no `rev`), a commit does not move the revision the next act is checked against. So that two acts from one observation still cannot both commit, an act that commits closes every claim taken before it at the same revision, unless it provably changed nothing: the revision did not move and either the handler reported `changed: []` or the revision is surface-owned (where a change would have bumped it). A waiting act whose claim was closed is `conflict` with `details: { expectedRev, currentRev }` (equal values) and a message that the application has not reported its new revision. A handler that threw closes the claims too, since it may have written. An act claimed after the commit, at the same revision, is accepted: the agent that observes again is never refused for a revision that cannot move.

**[S-ACT-11] The application's own precondition.** The surface's checks guard one surface; two instances (two tabs, two clients) of one application pass their own checks at the same revision. For state that lives on a server, the write itself is the gate. `act` passes the handler, the field's `set`, and the collection's `more` an `ActContext` `{ id, op, expectedRev, actor }` as the last argument, `expectedRev` being the revision the act presented, which equals the surface's revision when the handler starts; the handler carries it into its transaction or conditional write (`UPDATE ... WHERE revision = $expectedRev`). A handler whose write fails its precondition refuses by returning `{ conflict: { currentRev? } }` (the `conflict` member of `HandlerReport`) or throwing a `SurfaceConflict` (exported by `@axe/surface`, with an optional `currentRev`). `act` MUST then return `error` `conflict`, `retryable: true`, with `details: { expectedRev, currentRev, seq }` (`currentRev` as the handler reported it, else the surface's revision), record a refusal ([S-LED-4]), and adopt nothing from the report: no `rev`, no `changed`. The application's newer revision reaches the surface the way every revision does, through `setRev` on its next refresh. A handler that refuses MUST have written nothing.

**[S-REV-1]** The revision has one owner at a time, the surface's revision mode. In `surface` mode the application's state lives in the browser and it calls `bump()` after every change an agent could observe, whoever made it. In `app` mode the application has a revision of its own (a server counter) and adopts it with `setRev()`; the surface's stale check is then the first of two, and the server refuses a stale expected revision again, in the write itself ([S-ACT-11]). Unless pinned by `createSurface({ revision: "surface" | "app" })`, the mode follows the last call: `bump()` makes it `surface`, `setRev()` makes it `app`, and a fresh surface is `surface`; `seedRev()` leaves it alone. `revisionMode()` reports it. Freezes ([S-FRZ-2]) are the one behavior that differs by mode.

**[S-REV-2]** A retried act with the same `expectedRev` after a successful act MUST be refused (the revision moved), so a lost response cannot double-apply.

**[S-REV-3]** An act by one participant is visible to every other participant as a revision move; a human's change between an agent's observe and act is caught by [S-ACT-2].

**[S-REV-6] A stale report.** A `rev` a handler reports that is lower than the surface's revision when the handler settles (a newer revision arrived with `setRev` or `bump` while it ran) MUST NOT be adopted: the act is still `ok`, `output.rev` is the surface's current revision, `changed` is as reported, and the ledger entry carries the report as `ignoredRev`. A surface revision is an integer, so "lower" is well defined here; the protocol's revision stays opaque to agents ([P-LIVE-10]). Acts on one surface cannot complete out of order ([S-ACT-9]), so a stale report comes from a change made outside `act`.

**[S-REV-4]** Every act and every refusal is recorded in the surface's ledger (§5.1), so every change names who made it and at which revision, and every attempt that changed nothing says so.

### 5.1 The ledger

The first Surface left the record of who acted to the application, and its own critique was that the person never sees the agent's work: no ledger, no attribution, no replay. The surface is the one place every act passes through with the actor and both revisions in hand, so it keeps the record.

**[S-LED-1]** A surface MUST keep a ledger. Every call to `act` MUST append exactly one entry, whether the handler ran, the handler threw, or the act was refused. An entry is `{ seq, at, op, target, actor, revBefore, revAfter, status, text?, textHash?, changed, ignoredRev? }`: `at` is when it was written; `op` is the op as requested; `target` is the entry id the act resolved to, or the target as requested when it did not resolve; `text` is present when the request carried it and the surface's text policy keeps it ([S-PRV-2]), and `textHash` in its place under the `hash` policy; `ignoredRev` is present when the handler reported a revision the surface did not adopt ([S-REV-6]). The ledger records what reached `act`. So that it is a complete record of agent attempts, the checks of [S-ACT-3] that are the surface's own (`maxLength`, the field's schema, a required `confirm`) MUST run in the surface and not be duplicated in front of it: the bridge's input schemas describe the shape only ([S-AXE-6]). A request the protocol refuses before the surface (a malformed envelope, a `text` that is not a string, a stale `expectedRev` under [S-AXE-4]) is not in the ledger; it is in the server's request events.

**[S-LED-2]** `seq` starts at 1 and increases by one per entry; a `seq` is never reused, including after entries are dropped ([S-LED-6]). `ledger()` returns entries oldest first. `ledger({ since })` returns the entries whose `seq` is greater than `since`, so an agent that observed `ledgerSeq` can ask what happened after it looked. `actor` filters to one actor. `limit` caps the count: the oldest `limit` entries after `since` when `since` is given, so a reader can page forward, otherwise the newest `limit`.

**[S-LED-3]** `actor` is the actor the request carried (default `agent`; through AXE, [S-AXE-3]), recorded for refusals as much as for acts that ran, and it is the same value the handler received. The surface MUST NOT substitute its own idea of who acted.

**[S-LED-4]** A refusal MUST be recorded as a refusal: `status` is the error code the act returned (`conflict`, `not_found`, `invalid_input`, `unavailable`), `changed` is empty, and `revAfter` equals `revBefore`. A handler that threw is recorded with `status: "internal"` before the error propagates. A ledger MUST NOT present a refusal as a change, and MUST NOT omit it: an agent that was refused at rev 9 after observing rev 7 did nothing, and the record has to say so rather than leave a gap.

**[S-LED-5]** `revBefore` is the revision when the act was last checked (at execution for an act that waited, [S-ACT-9]; when it arrived otherwise) and `revAfter` the revision when the entry was written, after the handler ran and any `rev` it reported was adopted; `changed` is as in [S-ACT-1]. In `app` mode a handler that reports nothing leaves `revAfter` equal to `revBefore` and `changed` empty, since the application's revision arrives later; the record is still true. `status` is `ok` only when the handler ran and returned. Every act result carries the `seq` of its entry: `output.seq` for `ok`, `error.details.seq` for a refusal.

**[S-LED-6]** Retention MAY be bounded, and is by default: the surface keeps the most recent 1000 entries (`createSurface({ ledger: { max } })`). Dropping old entries MUST NOT renumber the rest, and `ledgerSeq` still reports the latest `seq`. The ledger is the application's live record, in memory; a durable audit trail is fed from the action events on the bridge ([S-AXE-5]) or by the application.

**[S-LED-7]** The ledger records acts made through the surface. A change made through the application's own controls appears as a revision move ([S-REV-3]), not as an entry, unless the application routes it through `act`.

**[S-REV-5]** The application MUST re-register (or `reconcile`) whenever anything that feeds an entry's `state`, `value`, or `edit` changes, including transient states: a save in flight, data loading, a modal open. An observation MUST reflect the application's state as of its `rev`. A surface that reports a control available while the application has disabled it is a defect; §5.1 is the shortest way to avoid it for a state that spans the surface.

### 5.2 Transient states

**[S-FRZ-1]** `freeze(reason)` marks the whole surface unavailable for a transient state and returns `thaw`. While frozen, every action reports `{ available: false, reason }` with the freeze's reason in place of its own state (and no `requires` or `handoff`), every field is read-only with that `reason`, no collection can load more, and no entry carries an `index`. Field values remain visible.

**[S-FRZ-2]** `observe()` carries a top-level `frozen: { reason }` while frozen and omits it otherwise, and `freeze` and `thaw` each notify `onChange` listeners (with `frozen` or `thawed` naming the reason). Whether the revision moves depends on the revision mode ([S-REV-1]). In `surface` mode `freeze` moves the revision and so does `thaw`: the freeze is a change of the state the surface counts. In `app` mode neither moves it: the revision is the application's, a freeze is not a change of the application's state, and a counter the surface moved on its own would drift ahead of the application's until its next `setRev`, refusing agents for a change that never happened. `registryVersion()` moves in neither mode.

**[S-FRZ-3]** An act during a freeze is `error` with `code: "unavailable"`, `retryable: true`, and `details.reason` (also `details.frozen`), whatever the entry's own state, and no handler runs. A numeric target during a freeze is `not_found`, since no row carries an index. An act planned before the freeze (its `expectedRev` observed before it) is, in `surface` mode, `conflict` under [S-ACT-2] both during and after the freeze, because the revision moved; in `app` mode it is `unavailable` while frozen and, after the thaw, accepted exactly when the application's revision did not move in between, and `conflict` when it did. In both modes an act planned during the freeze is `unavailable` until the thaw.

**[S-FRZ-4]** Freezes nest and are counted: the newest reason is the one reported, the surface thaws when the last freeze is released, and releasing the same freeze twice does nothing.

## 6. The seam: every control as an AXE action

The reviewer who designed Surface put it in one line: each available Surface entry can be published as an AXE action, so discovery, MCP, kinds, and the audit come for free. An agent reading the manifest or an MCP tool list sees "Draft Jahmyr Gibbs (RB)" and "Lock March", each with its own availability and reason, not one `act` tool with a `target` parameter. The first cut of this seam had that generic action; it is gone. A second granularity, a fixed set of parameterized actions the application declares ([S-AXE-10]), serves surfaces too large or too changeable for a tool per control; it shares the act, the revision, the ladder, the ledger, and the events, and it is not the generic action of v0.2: each action covers a declared group with metadata true of every member.

**[S-AXE-1]** `surfaceActions(surface, { namespace })` returns an action provider (the SDK's `ActionProvider`, registered with `addProvider`) whose set is, in order: `<ns>.observe` (read-only, `risk: none`, filtered and paged per [S-OBS-5], `display` over `entries`, and a dynamic affordance per actionable row that names the row's own action with `input: {}` and the observation's `rev` as the affordance's `expectedRev`), `<ns>.ledger` (read-only, `risk: none`, input `{ since?, limit? }`, output `{ ledgerSeq, entries }` with `display` over the entries), and one action per registered entry ([S-AXE-6]), in registration order. Contract v0.2's generic `<ns>.act` is removed; its `target`, `op`, and `input.expectedRev` have no successor, since each action names its entry and op and the revision travels on the envelope ([S-AXE-4]).

**[S-AXE-2]** Refusals map onto the ladder: an unavailable action with `requires` becomes `needs_input` with those `accepts` (and the declared `handoff` as `fallback` when it has a `url`); one with only a `handoff` that has a `url` becomes `needs_human`; a stale revision is `error` `conflict`; other refusals stay `error` with their reason. A `handoff` without a `url` ([S-AV-3]) never climbs the ladder, because `needs_human` requires a complete Handoff to resume from ([P-HAND-1]): the act stays `error` `unavailable` with `details.handoff` carrying the `reason`, `instructions`, and `whatAgentShouldSayToUser`, so the agent still learns what would unblock it. A refusal during a freeze ([S-FRZ-3]) is `error` `unavailable` with the freeze's reason and never climbs the ladder, since there is nothing to fulfill or hand off until the freeze lifts. On `/axe/continue`, the application's `fulfill` runs and the act is retried at the current revision; on `/axe/resume`, the act is retried at the current revision. The `state` a continuation or resume carries is the act request, with the entry id as `target`, so a later act lands on the same control whatever the action set has become.

**[S-AXE-3]** The `actor` recorded for an act that arrives through AXE is the authenticated `onBehalfOf` when there is one, else `agent`.

**[S-AXE-4]** Served through AXE, the surface's revision is the site's revision (protocol §21): the server is created with `revision: surface.currentRev`, so every envelope carries `rev`, the protocol's stale check ([P-LIVE-3]) runs before the handler, and `/axe/state` reports the revision. The revision the agent observed travels as the request envelope's `expectedRev` and nowhere else; an entry's action takes none in its `input`. The entry's action MUST pass the envelope's `expectedRev` to `act` unchanged. The protocol's check is the first gate and the surface's the second: two requests that pass the server's check at the same revision are ordered by [S-ACT-9], the later one re-checked at execution, so at most one of them commits when the first moves the revision; the handler then receives the same `expectedRev` for its own write ([S-ACT-11]). A request without `expectedRev` reaches the surface, which refuses it as `invalid_input` under [S-ACT-3]: every act presents the revision the agent saw.

**[S-AXE-5]** For every act that goes through an entry's action, `/axe/continue`, or `/axe/resume`, run or refused, the bridge MUST emit one action event (`EVENTS.md`, "Action events") named `surface.act`, with `action` the entry's action name, `status` and `errorCode` as the surface returned them, `actor` as recorded in the ledger, and `details: { seq, op, target, revBefore, revAfter, changed }`. Action events reach the server's `onActionEvent` sinks, not its analytics sinks, because they name the actor.

**[S-AXE-6] The action for an entry.** Its `title` is the entry `name` ([S-ENT-1]: the outcome, in the application's words). Its `description` is composed from the kind, the name, the `object`, and the effect, and stays within [P-ACT-7]'s bounds whatever the name's length; it does not carry the availability, which is live ([S-AXE-8]). `readOnly` is false for every kind: an action runs a handler, a field runs a setter, and a collection load changes the view. The `input` describes the shape of a request and nothing the surface checks itself, so every refusal of [S-ACT-3] is the surface's and is ledgered ([S-LED-1]). For an `action` entry, `idempotent`, `reversible`, and `risk` are the `effect`'s ([S-EFF-1]); `input` takes an optional boolean `confirm`, described as required to be `true`, when the effect declares `confirm`, and an optional `text` described by the effect's `text` prompt when it declares one. For a `field` entry, `idempotent` is true (setting the same text twice sets it once), `reversible` and `risk` are the bridge's field options (defaults false and `medium`), and `input` is `{ text }`, required, a string; the `maxLength` is named in its description and in the action's `description`, and `<ns>.observe` carries `edit.maxLength` and `edit.schema`. A read-only field keeps the same shape and is unavailable ([S-AXE-8]). For a `collection` entry that can load more, `idempotent` and `reversible` are false, `risk` is `low`, and `input` is empty; a collection without `more` has no action at all, since there is nothing to invoke, and keeps its row in `<ns>.observe` ([S-OBS-2]). Every entry action's `output` is the act output of [S-ACT-1]: `{ rev, id, actor, changed, seq }`. An action entry that declares a `handoff` with a `url` at registration declares `humanHandoff: { reasons: [handoff.reason] }`. The op the action performs is fixed by the entry: `TOGGLE` for a `switch`, `CLICK` for any other action role, `TYPE_TEXT` for a field, `SCROLL_DOWN` for a collection.

**[S-AXE-7] Names.** An entry's action is `<ns>.<slug>`. The plain slug of an id is the id lowercased, with every run of characters outside `a-z0-9` replaced by one hyphen, leading and trailing hyphens dropped, and `e-` prefixed when the result is empty or starts with a digit, so `pick:me:Gibbs` is `pick-me-gibbs`, `cell:2026-03` is `cell-2026-03`, and `2026-03` is `e-2026-03`. When the plain slug is `observe`, `ledger`, or `act`, or is already held by a different id, the slug is the plain slug, a hyphen, and a short hash of the exact id (FNV-1a, base 36), with a counter appended in the vanishing case of a second collision. The bridge keeps the map in both directions for its lifetime: `actionNameFor(entryId)` always returns the same name for the same id, an id that leaves and returns keeps its name, and `entryIdFor(actionName)` returns the exact id an action was issued for, or undefined for a name the bridge never issued. `entrySlug(id)` exposes the plain slug alone. Every name produced fits [P-ACT-1]'s pattern.

**[S-AXE-8] Availability.** Every entry action declares an `availability` hook ([P-LIVE-5]) that returns `surface.availability(id)` ([S-OBS-3]): an action's `state` with its `reason`, `requires`, and `handoff` (with or without a `url`); `{ available: false, reason }` for a read-only field; and, while the surface is frozen, `{ available: false, reason }` with the freeze's reason for every entry ([S-FRZ-1]). The manifest carries it as rendered and `/axe/state` serves it live, so an agent sees per control what it can do right now and why not the rest without calling `<ns>.observe`. The hook reads one entry; it MUST NOT take a whole observation, so `/axe/state` over hundreds of rows costs hundreds of lookups, not hundreds of tables.

**[S-AXE-9] Churn and scale.** The action set follows the registry: an entry registered or unregistered is in or out of the manifest, `/axe/state`, and the derived MCP tool list on the next request, without re-registering anything with the server. The set is rebuilt only when `registryVersion()` moved; a `bump` alone reuses the previous set, and within a rebuild an entry object already published keeps its action. Name lookup on invocation is by map, not by scan, so a surface with hundreds of rows invokes in constant time. An entry whose text fails the honesty lint ([P-HON-1]) at build time is not published as an action: the bridge reports it through its `onError` option (default: one warning per entry) and the entry keeps its row in `<ns>.observe`, without an affordance. A retried `idempotencyKey` for an action whose entry has since left the registry (a pick that leaves the board when drafted) is answered from the idempotency store by the server before the name is declared unknown ([P-IDEM-2]), so a lost response is still recoverable.

**[S-AXE-10] Parameterized domain actions.** `domainActions(surface, { namespace, domains })` returns an action provider whose set is, in order, `<ns>.observe`, `<ns>.ledger` (as in [S-AXE-1]), and one action per declared spec, named `<ns>.<spec name>`. The set is fixed when the provider is created: it does not change as entries register and unregister, and the provider's `onChange` never fires. Each spec carries a `title`, a `description`, `covers(entry)`, and `risk`, `idempotent`, `reversible`, and optionally `auth` and `humanHandoff`. An entry belongs to the first spec whose `covers` returns true, decided once per registered entry object, subject to [S-AXE-11]. Without `domains` the specs are `set-field` (fields; the bridge's field risk, idempotent), `run-control` (action entries; `risk: high`, not idempotent, not reversible), and `load-more` (collections; `risk: low`). The action's input is `{ entryId, text?, confirm? }`, `entryId` a non-empty string; its output is the act output of [S-ACT-1]. The act it performs is `surface.act` with `target` the `entryId`, the op the entry's own action would use ([S-AXE-6]), the envelope's `expectedRev` unchanged ([S-AXE-4]), and the actor of [S-AXE-3]; refusals map onto the ladder as in [S-AXE-2], with the saved act's `target` the entry id, and every act emits the event of [S-AXE-5] with `action` the domain action's name. So the revision, the ledger, and the events are the same in both granularities for the same act. `<ns>.observe` gives every actionable row whose entry is covered an affordance naming the covering action, with `input: { entryId }` and the observation's `rev` as `expectedRev`. `actionFor(entryId)` returns the covering action's name and `covered(name)` the covered ids.

**[S-AXE-11] Metadata true of every covered entry.** A spec's `risk`, `idempotent`, and `reversible` are published for the whole group, so they MUST hold for each entry it covers ([P-HON-7]). An entry whose own risk ([S-AXE-6]) is higher than the spec's, that is not reversible while the spec is declared reversible, or not idempotent while the spec is declared idempotent, is not covered. Neither is an entry whose `name` or effect `text` fails the honesty lint ([P-HON-1]). Such an entry is reported once per entry object through `onError`, keeps its row in `<ns>.observe`, and has no affordance; an act naming it through a domain action is refused under [S-AXE-12].

**[S-AXE-12] Routing and availability of a parameterized action.** An `entryId` that names a registered entry covered by a different spec, or by none, is `invalid_input` at `/entryId` before the surface, as an unknown action name would be, and is not ledgered; so is a field entry without `text` (the per-control action's schema requires it). An `entryId` no entry carries goes to `act`, which refuses it as `not_found` and records it ([S-LED-1]). The action's `availability` hook ([P-LIVE-5]) reads one entry at a time and never a whole observation: `{ available: true }` while at least one covered entry can be acted on ([S-OBS-3]); otherwise `{ available: false, reason }` with the reason every covered entry reports when they all report the same one (a freeze, [S-FRZ-1]), else the spec's `idleReason`. [S-AUTH-5] applies unchanged: for a caller the runtime has not identified, a protected action reports `{ available: true }` without reading a surface. Per-entry availability, `requires`, and `handoff` are reported on the entry's row of `<ns>.observe` and in the refusal of an act on it, as for its own action.

**[S-AXE-13] Both granularities side by side.** `surfaceActions` and `domainActions` MAY serve the same surface on one server under different namespaces; their acts share the surface's revision, act ordering ([S-ACT-9]), and ledger. Per-control names are stable per entry id for the life of a bridge ([S-AXE-7]); domain action names are the application's and do not depend on entries. Resolvers and templates ([S-AUTH-4]) work the same way in both; with a resolver the coverage of an entry is decided on the caller's own surface.

### 6.1 Authorization and privacy

A surface holds field values, domain objects, and the free text agents typed. In the browser that is the page's own data ([S-DOM-2]); served over HTTP it is published to whoever can reach the server. The rules below make the second an explicit decision. They do not change [S-RULE-1]: the surface still decides nothing about who may do what inside the application; the bridge declares which callers the AXE runtime lets through at all, and the runtime enforces it (protocol §16).

**[S-AUTH-1] The bridge declares who may call.** Every action `surfaceActions` generates carries an `auth` mode (protocol [P-AUTH-1]), from the `auth` option: `user` (the default), `site`, or `none`. A server rejects an action whose mode is not in its `auth.modes`, so a server that lists no `user` mode, including one created with the protocol's public default `{ modes: ["none"] }`, refuses a bridge created without options on its first request instead of serving it. `auth: "none"` is the only way to make a surface public over HTTP, and it is written by the integrator. A mode outside the three is an error when the bridge is created.

**[S-AUTH-2] Read and mutate.** The `read` option sets the mode of `<ns>.observe` and `<ns>.ledger` (default: `auth`). `authFor(entry)` sets one entry's action's mode, and returns undefined to keep `auth`. A public read side over protected controls (`read: "none"`) publishes every value `observe` returns; it is a choice, not a default.

**[S-AUTH-3] Authorization is the runtime's refusal, never a reason.** A caller the runtime cannot admit is refused by the runtime before any bridge handler runs: `unauthorized` without credentials (or `needs_human` with `login_required` when a `user` action and a login page are configured, [P-AUTH-4]), `forbidden` for a site key outside its allowlist ([P-AUTH-2]). Nothing reaches the surface, the ledger, or the action events. An entry's `state.reason` stays the application's capability report; the bridge never writes an authorization message into it, and never answers an unauthorized caller with `unavailable`.

**[S-AUTH-4] One surface per caller.** A server that hosts the surfaces of many users or tenants passes a resolver, `(context) => Surface | null`, in place of the surface, with a `template` surface. The context is what the runtime established: `identity`, `tenant`, `siteKey`, `request`. The template's entries define the action set for every caller: the manifest, `/axe/state`, and the MCP tool list show the template's names and never the union of callers' entries, so no caller's names or ids reach another caller or an anonymous reader. Each request resolves the caller's surface once; `observe`, `ledger`, and every act run against it, an act on an id the caller's surface does not hold is `not_found` from that surface, and a caller's revision is its own surface's. A resolver that returns nothing refuses the call with `unauthorized` for an unidentified caller and `forbidden` for an identified one, saying nothing about other callers. `scopedSurfaces({ create, key?, max?, idleMs? })` builds a resolver whose default key is the tenant id and the principal (the user's `onBehalfOf`, else `site:<key id>`), creates a surface on a scope's first request, and evicts the least recently used surface beyond `max` (default 1000) and any surface idle longer than `idleMs` (default 30 minutes). With a resolver the server's `revision` option is not the surface's, since the protocol's revision provider does not see the caller: leave it off and the surface's own guard ([S-ACT-2]) checks `expectedRev`, or give a provider that resolves the caller itself.

**[S-AUTH-5] Anonymous views read nothing protected.** The manifest's `availability` is computed for an anonymous caller ([P-LIVE-7]) and `/axe/state` for the caller who asks. For an entry whose mode is not `none`, the bridge's availability hook returns `{ available: true }` without reading any surface when the runtime established no identity: the protocol's report for an action that declares no availability, meaning the surface makes no claim to that caller, while `auth` says who may call. It reads the caller's surface once the caller is identified. A `site` caller without a user identity is unidentified here, since the availability context carries no site key.

**[S-AUTH-6] Actor and principal.** The recorded `actor` is still [S-AXE-3]'s; scoping is by principal and tenant, never by the actor string, which an application may map to a display name.

**[S-PRV-1] Sensitive fields.** A field entry MAY declare `sensitive: true`. Its row in `observe()` carries `sensitive: true` and no `value`, locally, through `expose`, and through `<ns>.observe`; `describeEntry` says `value hidden`; the DOM mirror never carries a value for any entry ([S-DOM-1]); the ledger keeps none of its text ([S-PRV-2]). The application's own `objects` are its responsibility: keep private data out of `setObjects` or protect the read side.

**[S-PRV-2] Ledger text policy.** `createSurface({ ledger: { text, redact } })`: `text` is `keep` (the default), `hash` (the entry carries `textHash`, the hex SHA-256 of the text, and no `text`), or `omit`. `redact(text, entryId)` rewrites kept text, and returning undefined drops it. The policy applies to every entry the ledger writes: acts that ran, refusals, and handlers that threw. A sensitive field's text is never kept under any policy, and neither is the text of an act whose target did not resolve to an entry while any sensitive entry is registered, since a refusal before resolution cannot say which row it meant. A hash links equal texts; it does not hide guessable text.

**[S-PRV-3] Action events.** A `surface.act` event ([S-AXE-5]) carries no text and no value. The bridge's `redactEvent(details, { action, entryId })` rewrites its `details` before emission, for run and refused acts alike, for ids that are themselves personal.

## 7. Browser agents

Browser-driving agents and screen readers read a control's accessible name, not its registration. Surface mirrors each entry onto the element that receives its interaction, so the page, `observe()`, and the screen reader all read the same outcome-stating name. Two of three gains in the first trial came from the names alone.

**[S-DOM-1] The DOM mirror.** The rules below hold for every entry that has a control on screen.

1. An entry's `name` states the outcome in the application's own words ("Draft Jahmyr Gibbs (RB)", "Lock March", "Load more players"). It MUST NOT name the widget ("button", "Draft button"), repeat the `role`, or carry the state ("disabled", "already drafted"): the role is `role`, the state is `state`. The same string is the control's accessible name and the `name` in `observe()`.
2. The element that receives the entry's interaction (the button, the input, the scrolling container) carries these attributes, and no other element in the document carries its `data-surface-id`:

   | Attribute             | Value                                    | When                                     |
   | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
   | `aria-label`          | the entry `name`                         | always                                   |
   | `data-surface-id`     | the entry `id`                           | always; exactly one element per entry    |
   | `data-surface-kind`   | `action`, `field`, or `collection`       | always                                   |
   | `data-surface-object` | the entry `object`                       | when the entry has one                   |
   | `aria-disabled`       | `true`                                   | an action whose `state` is not available |
   | `aria-readonly`       | `true`                                   | a read-only field                        |
   | `data-surface-reason` | the `reason`, in the application's words | with `aria-disabled` or `aria-readonly`  |

   Collections carry the common set only.

3. Two helpers produce that set. `surfaceAttributes(entry)` returns it as strings from a registered entry or an observed row, read from the entry alone: it reports the entry's own `state` and `edit` and knows nothing of a freeze, and it returns only what to set. `surface.attributesFor(id)` returns `{ set, remove }` for a registered entry from its live availability ([S-OBS-3]): during a freeze `set` carries `aria-disabled` or `aria-readonly` with the freeze's reason, and `remove` lists every attribute of `SURFACE_ATTRIBUTES` (the table above, exported from `@axe/surface`) that is not in `set`, so a renderer that applies both never leaves the previous render's reason on a control that became available. `remove` never names `aria-label` or `data-surface-id` for a registered entry, since both are always in `set`, so a renderer that keeps its own `aria-label` need only skip setting it. An application that re-renders controls while the surface lives MUST use `attributesFor` or an equivalent that reads `availability(id)`; `surfaceAttributes` is for a one-time render. `useSurfaceProps(entry, deps)` (`@axe/surface/react`) registers the entry like `useSurface` and returns the live set as props, with `aria-disabled` and `aria-readonly` as booleans, re-rendering when it changes (it reads the surface through `subscribe` [S-OBS-4], so a change made outside the component, by another writer or by `reconcile`, reaches the control too). An application that uses any of these MUST render every attribute returned on that element, MUST remove the attributes named in `remove`, and MUST NOT replace `aria-label` or `data-surface-id` with other values. An application that renders by hand SHOULD carry the same set.
4. The mirror reports; it does not disable [S-RULE-1]. The helper sets `aria-disabled`, never `disabled`, so the application keeps its own disabling logic, and an `aria-disabled` control stays focusable, so a screen reader reaches the reason beside it.

**[S-DOM-2]** `expose(window)` publishes `observe`, `act`, `ledger`, and `rev` at `window.__surface` for agents that drive the page. This is browser-local: whatever runs in the page can already read what the page renders and call its handlers, so `expose` adds no reader the page did not have, and it needs no `auth`. It is not equivalent to serving the surface over HTTP, where §6.1 applies. `expose` returns the same `observe()` as the surface, so a sensitive field's value is withheld there too ([S-PRV-1]).

**[S-DOM-3] The selector contract.** `surfaceSelector(id)` returns `[data-surface-id="<id>"]` with the id as a double-quoted CSS string: backslashes and double quotes are escaped with a backslash, a newline becomes `\a `, and everything else (colons, spaces, dots, brackets) is left as it is. On a page that follows [S-DOM-1], `document.querySelector(surfaceSelector(row.id))` MUST return the control for `row.id` from any observation, whatever the id contains. Ids are stable [S-ACT-5], so a selector computed from one observation still finds the control after the revision moves; a stale selector is never an error, a stale act is [S-ACT-2].

**[S-DOM-4] One-line description.** `describeEntry(entry)` returns one line of plain text: the `name`, a colon, then the entry's availability in the application's words (`available`, or `unavailable, <reason>`; for a field `editable` or `read only, <reason>`, followed by its value and length; for a collection what is loaded and whether it can load more), and for an action a semicolon and its effect (`mutates <state>`, `idempotent` when set, `reversible` or `not reversible`, `risk <level>`, `confirm required` when set): "Draft Jahmyr Gibbs (RB): unavailable, already drafted by another team; mutates draft, reversible, risk medium". It is agent-read text [S-AV-4]. Applications MAY render it as `aria-description` on the control or log it beside each act; the helper does not render it by default.

## 8. WebMCP: the browser's own agent

WebMCP is a draft browser API (W3C Web Machine Learning Community Group, draft report of 17 September 2026) through which a page registers tools for an agent built into the browser: `document.modelContext.registerTool({ name, description, inputSchema, execute, annotations }, { signal })`, unregistered by aborting the signal. As of September 2026 Chrome offers it behind a flag and in an origin trial (from Chrome 149); other browsers do not ship it. `@axe/surface/webmcp` maps a surface onto it. It is optional and outside the browser core: `@axe/surface` and `@axe/surface/react` never import it.

**[S-WEB-1] Feature detection.** `exposeWebMcp(surface, options)` registers nothing unless it finds an object with `registerTool` (by default `document.modelContext`), and then returns a handle with `supported: false` and the reason. The page, its controls, and `expose` ([S-DOM-2]) behave the same with or without it. The adapter reaches the browser API in one place, so a change in the draft changes that place only.

**[S-WEB-2] What is offered.** One tool reads the surface (`<prefix>observe`, read-only: the observation of [S-OBS-1] as text and as structured content). One tool per entry that can be acted on: an action, a field that is not `sensitive`, a collection with `more`. A sensitive field ([S-PRV-1]) gets no tool, and no tool description or result carries its value. The tool's description is `describeEntry` ([S-DOM-4]) plus what the tool does; its input schema is a JSON Schema object with an optional `expectedRev`, `text` for a field (with the field's schema and `maxLength`), and a required `confirm` for an action whose effect asks for confirmation. An action that is not reversible carries the `consequentialHint` annotation. Tool names are the prefix (default `surface_`) and the id with every character outside letters, digits, `_`, and `-` replaced by `_`; ids that map to the same name, or to the observe tool's, get an 8-hex-digit hash of the id appended. Names are stable for an id.

**[S-WEB-3] Availability follows the surface.** The adapter follows `subscribe` ([S-OBS-4]) and re-registers a tool when its name, description, schema, or annotations changed, and only then. An unavailable entry is registered with its reason, `requires`, and `handoff` in the description (`unavailable: "describe"`, the default) or left out until it is available (`"omit"`). A freeze makes every entry unavailable with the freeze's reason ([S-FRZ-1]).

**[S-WEB-4] Acts go through the surface.** A tool call is `surface.act` with the entry's id, the entry's op, the tool's `text` and `confirm`, and `expectedRev` from the call, or, when the call gives none, the surface's revision at the moment of the call. The surface's revision guard, availability, confirmation, ledger, and handler apply unchanged ([S-ACT-2], [S-LED-1]); the ledger's `actor` is the adapter's (`webmcp` unless set). The result is the act's envelope as structured content with a line of text, and `isError` when it is not `ok`.

**[S-WEB-5] Browser-local.** Like `expose`, WebMCP exposure adds no reader the page did not have: the browser's agent acts in the page with the user's session. It is not a way to reach the surface from a server; server-side agents use the AXE bridge (§6) and its authorization (§6.1).

## Provenance

v0.7 came from the same review's item on existing agent interfaces (`docs/review/2026-09-24/14-agent-interface-adapters.md`): WebMCP gives the browser's agent a tool API of its own, and a surface already has everything a tool needs (names, reasons, schemas, a guarded act).

[S-ACT-6] to [S-ACT-8], [S-REV-5], and the rules of §5.2 came from two defects found on a real planning application in the first staging trial, both in the seam between Surface and the application: an act whose `target` was `null` matched a row that was not actionable, and the table kept reporting cells editable while the application had frozen editing during a save, so an act ran into the frozen UI. Both were fixed in the application and became rules of the contract here, so the next application does not find them again.

v0.6 came from the same review's item on mounted React behavior (`docs/review/2026-09-24/11-react-lifecycle.md`). Mounted tests under jsdom showed that `useSurface` kept the old id registered after an id change and removed the wrong one at unmount, registered nothing in a new target surface, and ran the handler from the render that last changed `deps`; and that `useSurfaceProps` never heard a registration made outside the component, because registration did not notify. Ordering under ordinary updates, Strict Mode without an id change, freezes, and the removal of a stale reason already held.

v0.5 came from the engineering review of 2026-09-24, which found every generated action inheriting `auth: "none"` and a server configured with `none` and `user` modes serving observe, the ledger (with typed text), and every mutation to an anonymous caller (`docs/review/2026-09-24/05-surface-authorization.md`).

v0.4 came from integrating the SDK into that application rather than porting it: a browser bundle that failed on a Node built-in and carried Zod for a schema it never used ([S-ENT-3]); freezes that moved a revision the server owned ([S-FRZ-2], [S-FRZ-3]); `changed` that was always empty and a ledger whose `revAfter` never moved, because the revision arrived after the handler ([S-ACT-1], [S-LED-5]); a DOM mirror that said editable while `observe()` said frozen, and could not clear a stale reason ([S-DOM-1]); a collection with nothing to load published as an action that could never run ([S-AXE-6]); a human-only step with no page to point at, reportable only as a bare reason ([S-AV-3], [S-AXE-2]); and over-length text refused in front of the surface, so the attempt never reached the ledger ([S-LED-1]). Each was first shimmed in the application; each is now the contract.

v0.5 came from a review that ran two asynchronous acts presenting the same `expectedRev` against one surface: both passed the check, both ran, and both returned `ok`, because the check ran before the handler and nothing held the revision while the handler awaited. A preflight comparison is not concurrency control, and the `app` revision mode did not change that. The surface now orders its own acts and checks a waiting act again at execution ([S-ACT-9], [S-ACT-10]); across instances, where no local queue can help, the handler gets the presented revision for its conditional write and a way to say it failed ([S-ACT-11]); and a handler's late answer can no longer roll the revision back ([S-REV-6]).
